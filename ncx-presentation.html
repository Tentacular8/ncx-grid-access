DG Network · the problem

What that costs us

The gap isn't one missing control. It's that access and the equipment itself are both defended by the same single thin layer.

Access

No real control over who reaches site equipment

Being on the corporate network is treated as authorization. There is no per-user, per-device decision anywhere in the path.

Equipment

No security portfolio at the sites

Site devices carry no protection of their own beyond a local login. Nothing inspects, segments, or monitors what happens once traffic arrives.

Perimeter

Port forwards and a general access list

Traffic to and from the sites is permitted broadly rather than explicitly. The rule set describes convenience, not intent.

Where we are going

A secure infrastructure across the Grid — a DG Network solution paired with OT monitoring that detects and protects against cybersecurity threats, while keeping the organization simplified and secure.

Site · segmented 07 / 08

What we need to decide

Decisions and asks

Two decisions unlock the design. Neither is technically difficult; both need an owner and a budget line. The first can wait a little. The second gates half the security model.

Decision 1 · gateway

On premises or SaaS

Both deliver the same security model. The difference is what we operate and what we can support later.

  • On premises — data plane stays in our building, FIPS available, keeps the door open for a future CIP Medium site. We size, patch and make it redundant.
  • SaaS — nothing in the rack, clientless vendor access included, redundancy is the vendor's problem. Fits non-CIP and CIP Low sites.
Decision 2 · site switches

Access to existing switches, or replace them

The switch is the layer 2 boundary between device groups; the modem governs routing between them and remains the only path to the core. With a single trunk port in use, there is no VLAN separation at a site without a switch we can configure.

  • Gain access — cheapest path where the installed switch is already managed. Needs credentials, an owner, and a VLAN standard that covers unused ports and the native VLAN.
  • Replace — required where switches are unmanaged. Capital cost per site, and the only way to get separation at those locations.
  • This one is on the critical path. Slide 6's boundary protection lands with the modem alone. Everything on slide 7 waits on this decision.
What this buys us

Boundary protection from the modem, internal segmentation from the switch, and an OT monitoring feed with something meaningful to watch — the layered posture the Grid does not have today.
